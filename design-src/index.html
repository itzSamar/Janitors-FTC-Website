engineering notebook

engineering
portfolio

How we got from a whiteboard sketch to a robot that mostly works, including the parts we got wrong twice. This is the short version of our notebook.

Ø1
Iteration

what we changed

drivetrain

Problem — Started with a six-wheel tank drive. It was simple but we lost every pushing match in practice and could not line up on the scoring position.

Change — Switched to four-motor mecanum. We gave up some pushing power for the ability to strafe into place.

v1.0
intake

Problem — First intake used rigid 3D-printed rollers. They either missed the game piece or jammed it against the frame.

Change — Moved to compliant wheels at 45A durometer. They deform around the piece, so approach angle stopped mattering.

v2.0
lift

Problem — A single-stage slide could not reach the top scoring level without making the robot too tall to start.

Change — Went to a two-stage cascade slide. Same start height, twice the reach, about 80 g heavier.

v3.0
auto

Problem — Dead-reckoning autos drifted a few centimetres per run and missed by the fourth cycle.

Change — Added AprilTag localisation to correct position at the start of each path. Reliability went from about 60% to 94%.

v4.0

Inspection

inspection

eye protection

Safety glasses are worn by everyone in the shop, no exceptions, including visitors.

power tools

Only members who have been checked out on a tool may use it, and never alone in the shop.

battery handling

Batteries are charged on a fireproof mat, never left overnight, and inspected before every match.

pit conduct

Robot is powered down and the battery is disconnected before anyone reaches inside it.
